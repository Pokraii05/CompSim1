    \section*{Problem 10: Statement}\label{problem-10-statement}

Modify your MOnte Carlo computer program of Problem 8 for the simulation
of the 1D Ising model with

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\tightlist
\item
  periodic boundary conditions and
\item
  the Glauber update algorithm
\end{enumerate}

Determine as in Problem 8 the mean energy, specific heat, magnetisation
and susceptibility for \(N=10,100,1000,10000\) spins and different
temperatures in the inverse temperature range
\(\beta = J/(k_bT)=0,...,2\). Check your simulations by comparison with
the exact comparisons for the mean energy and specific heat. Compare
also with the numerical results of Problem 8.

    \section*{Problem 10: Solution}\label{problem-10-solution}

    We modify the program of Problem 8 in two ways.

(a) With periodic boundary conditions the spins \(s_1\) and \(s_N\) are
neighbours as well, so the energy is

\[
E=-J\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_is_{i+1}, \qquad s_{N+1}=s_1 \tag{4}
\]

and in the energy change of a spin flip

\[
\Delta E=2Js_i(s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}) \tag{5}
\]

every spin has two neighbours, with \(s_0=s_N\) and \(s_{N+1}=s_1\).

(b) In the Glauber algorithm a proposed flip is accepted with probability

\[
p=\frac{e^{-\beta\Delta E}}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta E}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta\Delta E}} \tag{6}
\]

instead of the Metropolis probability \(\min(1,e^{-\beta\Delta E})\).

We measure the same quantities as in Problem 8, eqs. (9) and (10):

\[
e=\frac{\langle E\rangle}{N}, \qquad c=\frac{\beta^2}{N}\left(\langle E^2\rangle-\langle E\rangle^2\right), \qquad m=\frac{\langle |M|\rangle}{N}, \qquad \chi=\frac{\beta}{N}\langle M^2\rangle \tag{7}
\]

We add the two modifications as options \texttt{periodic} and
\texttt{glauber} to the program of Problem 8, so that with both set to
\texttt{False} it is the program of Problem 8.

    For periodic boundary conditions the partition function is, as derived

\[

\]

\(\langle E\rangle=-\frac{1}{Z}\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\beta}\) and
\(\langle E^2\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial\beta^2}\),
after dividing numerator and denominator by \(\lambda_+^N\) and writing
\(t=\tanh(\beta J)\),

\[
e=\frac{\langle E\rangle}{N}=-J\frac{t+t^{N-1}}{1+t^N} \tag{9}
\]

\[
\langle E^2\rangle=J^2N\frac{(N-1)\left(t^2+t^{N-2}\right)+1+t^N}{1+t^N}, \qquad c=\frac{\beta^2}{N}\left(\langle E^2\rangle-\langle E\rangle^2\right) \tag{10}
\]

    As we can see on Fig. 3 and Fig. 4, the Monte Carlo results for \(e\)
and \(c\) agree with the exact results eqs. (9) and (10) for all \(N\)
and both algorithms. Metropolis and Glauber give the same results, since
both sample the same Boltzmann distribution. The only difference is
\(m\) at \(\beta=0\) (Fig. 5), where the Metropolis value is too small
for the same reason as in Problem 8. The Glauber algorithm accepts a
flip at \(\beta=0\) only with probability \(1/2\) and does not have this
problem.

Compared with Problem 8, for \(N=10\) periodic boundary conditions give
a lower energy and larger \(c\), \(m\) and \(\chi\), since the chain has
\(N\) instead of \(N-1\) bonds and no free ends. This boundary effect
decreases like \(1/N\): the energies differ by about \(1\%\) for
\(N=100\) and agree for \(N=10000\).
