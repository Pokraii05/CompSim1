    \section*{Problem 5: Statement}\label{problem-5-statement}

Use your computer program of problem 3 for the exact enumeration of all
states of the 1D Ising model with free boundary conditions (without
external field, i.e.~h=0) to determine the density of the states
\(\Omega (E,M)\) for \(N=20\) spins. Here E is the energy of a spin
configuration \({s_1,s_2,...,s_N}\) and \(M = \sum_{i=1}^{N} s_i\) the
associated magnetization

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\alph{enumi})}
\item
  Use \(\Omega (E,M)\) to find \(\langle E \rangle(T)\) and the specific
  heat \(C(T)\) in the field free case \(h = 0\). Plot both functions in
  the interval \(T \in (0,2]\).
\item
  Determine \(\langle E \rangle (T)\) and \(\langle M \rangle(T)\) by
  means of \(\Omega (E,M)\) for \(h \neq 0\) and plot both quantities at
  \(h = 0.5\) for \(T \in (0,2]\)
\item
  The purely energetic density of states \(\Omega (E)\), where one does
  not distinguish between different magnetizations, can be calculated
  exactly for an arbitrary number of spins N. Derive this result and
  verify it with the exact enumeration data.
\end{enumerate}

    In this task however, we are asked to determine the density of states
\(\Omega (E,M)\). From the lecture and previous homeworks we know that
the partition function for the 1D Ising model with free boundary
conditions is given by eq. (1).

\[
Z(\beta)=\sum_{\{s\}} e^{-\beta E(\{s\})} \tag{1}
\]

Furthermore, we know that eq. (1) can also be rewritten as

\[
Z(\beta)=\sum_{E} \Omega(E) e^{-\beta E} \tag{2}
\]

where \(\Omega(E)\) is the density of states. In the current exact
enumeration code we calculate E for every spin configuration, and in
order to determine \(\Omega(E)\) we can just save every E in a
dictionary using a conditional statement: If E is not in dictionary, add
E to dictionary with key 1, else if E is in dictionary, add 1 to the
already existing key for E.

Since we know that there are 20 spins, and each of them can take one of
two values, the total amount of possible combinations of the 20 spins is
\(2^{20}\). This means that

\[
\sum_{E} \Omega(E) = 2^{20} = 1048576 \tag{3}
\]

Using this we can check if the determined \(\Omega(E)\) is correct.

    \subsection*{Part (a)}

Furthermore, we are asked to find \(\langle E \rangle(T)\) and the
specific heat \(C(T)\) for \(h=0\) using our predetermined
\(\Omega (E,M)\)

From Homework 1 we know that
\(\langle E\rangle = -\dfrac{\partial \ln Z}{\partial \beta}\) and the
specific heat is \(C = \dfrac{d \langle E \rangle}{dT}\).

    If we use our formulation of the partition function Z given by eq. (2)
we can rewrite \(\langle E \rangle\) as follows:

    \[
\begin{aligned}
\langle E\rangle &= -\dfrac{\partial \ln Z}{\partial \beta} \\
&= \frac{-1}{\sum_{E} \Omega(E) e^{-\beta E}} \frac{\partial }{\partial \beta} \sum_{E} \Omega(E) e^{-\beta E} = \frac{\sum_{E} E\Omega(E) e^{-\beta E}}{\sum_{E} \Omega(E) e^{-\beta E}}
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]

    We can find \(\langle E \rangle(T)\) and \(\langle M \rangle(T)\)
similarly as in part (a). The partition function is

\[
Z(T,h)=\sum_{E,M}\Omega(E,M)e^{-\beta(E-hM)} \tag{7}
\]

Then \(\langle E \rangle(T)\) and \(\langle M \rangle(T)\) are as
follows:

\[
\langle E\rangle(T)
=
\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{E,M}E\Omega(E,M)e^{-\beta(E-hM)}
}{
\displaystyle\sum_{E,M}\Omega(E,M)e^{-\beta(E-hM)}
} \tag{8}
\]

\[
\langle M\rangle(T)
=
\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial h}
=
\frac{
\displaystyle\sum_{E,M}M\Omega(E,M)e^{-\beta(E-hM)}
}{
\displaystyle\sum_{E,M}\Omega(E,M)e^{-\beta(E-hM)}
} \tag{9}
\]
